## What I have learned so far {#lessons}

**I'm still early, but five lessons stand out, and each one comes with a number.**

1. **Price sets everything.** In the illustrative example, paying 4.0x passes the bank test at 1.33x, and paying 4.5x fails it at 1.19x (the 1.25x test is one reading of the SBA rules; confirm with your lender).
2. **Verify the earnings.** $250K of earnings that aren't real would cost $1.0M at 4.0x (illustrative).
3. **Day 1 cash is a separate bill.** If the seller keeps the receivables, the illustrative buyer needs about $600K of cash or credit, nearly 3 times their own $213K.
4. **The handover and the people decide a lot.** One law firm reads the new SBA rules as letting a seller consult for up to 24 months, and I'd plan the handover around that (confirm with your lender).
5. **Leverage makes good outcomes great and bad outcomes total.** In the illustrative plan, a sale in year 6 returns 21.0x the buyer's cash, while a 35% drop in earnings wipes it out (proceeds only; ignores fees and taxes).

Where I'm heading: keep searching, keep testing my two industries against the buy box, and get better at the part I find hardest, raising equity. If you've bought a company with an SBA loan, I'd love to compare notes.

Thank you to the Searchfunder community, and to the sellers, brokers and bankers who took my calls, for taking me from 0 to 0.5. Still a long way to go.
